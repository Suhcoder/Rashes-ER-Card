Patient Presents with RASH
INITIAL ASSESSMENT (Immediate Actions)
  • ABCs & Vital Signs: Ensure stability (Airway, Breathing, Circulation).
  • Brief Visual Exam: Gross appearance, distribution, signs of distress?
  • Quick History: Onset? Sudden/Gradual? Associated Sx (Fever, SOB, Swelling, Pain)? New Meds/Exposures? Allergies?
SUSPECT LIFE-THREATENING CONDITION?
(Signs: Airway compromise, Hypotension, Diffuse Erythroderma + Fever, Mucosal Involvement + Blistering/Sloughing, Purpura + Fever/Sepsis, Rapidly Spreading Painful Erythema)
IMMEDIATE ACTION - LIFE THREATS
  • Anaphylaxis/Angioedema: IM Epinephrine (0.3-0.5mg Ad; 0.01mg/kg Peds), O2, IV Fluids, Antihistamines (H1+H2), Steroids. SECURE AIRWAY EARLY.
  • SJS/TEN: STOP Offending Drug, Fluid Resuscitation, Wound Care, Pain Mgmt, Urgent Derm/Burn Consult, +/- IVIG/Cyclosporine.
  • Meningococcemia/Purpura Fulminans: IV Fluids, Broad IV Abx (Ceftriaxone+Vanc), Cultures, LP (if stable), Supportive Care, Droplet Precautions.
  • Necrotizing Fasciitis: IV Fluids, Broad IV Abx (incl. Anaerobes/MRSA), URGENT Surgical Consult & Debridement, Pain Control, Tetanus.
  • DRESS Syndrome: STOP Offending Drug, Supportive Care, Systemic Corticosteroids (often high dose), Consult Derm/Medicine.
FOCUSED HISTORY & EXAM
  • History: Itch? Pain? Fever? Chronicity? Triggers? Meds (New/Old)? Travel? Sick contacts? Atopy Hx? Immune status?
  • Exam: Morphology (Macules, Papules, Vesicles, Bullae, Pustules, Wheals, Plaques, Purpura, Targets?), Distribution (Localized, Generalized, Dermatomal?), Mucosal involvement?
PRIMARY SYMPTOM: PRURITUS (ITCH)?
Morphology Suggests:
Urticaria / Angioedema (Simple): Antihistamines (H1: Cetirizine, Loratadine, Fexo; +/- Diphenhydramine). H2 Blockers adj. Steroids (severe/refractory).
Contact Dermatitis (Allergic/Irritant): Topical Corticosteroids (Potency varies). Systemic Steroids (severe). Avoid trigger. Cool compress. Antihistamines (itch).
Eczema Flare (Atopic Derm): Topical Corticosteroids, Emollients (thick). Antihistamines (night itch). +/- Abx (secondary infection). Wet wraps (severe).
Scabies: Permethrin Cream / Oral Ivermectin. Treat contacts. Wash linens (hot). Antihistamines (itch persists). Topical steroids (residual rash).
Drug Rxn / Viral Exanthem (Morbilliform): Antihistamines. Topical Steroids (inflamed). STOP non-essential new meds. Supportive care.
General Pruritus Mgmt: Cool Compresses, Emollients, Keep Nails Short. Consider Doxepin (refractory - use carefully).
Morphology / Features Suggest:
Cellulitis / Erysipelas: Antibiotics (PO/IV for Skin Flora +/- MRSA). Elevation. Mark borders. Pain control. Consider Imaging (abscess?).
Erythema Multiforme (Minor): Symptomatic (Antihistamines if itchy, Topical Steroids rarely needed). +/- Oral Acyclovir (if HSV trigger & early). Rule out SJS.
Purpura / Petechiae (NON-BLANCHING): **INVESTIGATE!** CBC, Plt, Coags, CMP, UA, ESR/CRP. Cultures (if febrile). Treat Cause. Consults (Heme/Rheum/ID). Consider HSP (kids).
Viral Exanthem (Morbilliform): Symptomatic (Antipyretics: Acetaminophen/Ibuprofen). Supportive care, hydration. Reassurance.
Fixed Drug Eruption: STOP offending drug. Topical corticosteroids (symptomatic relief). Document agent clearly.
General Non-Pruritic Mgmt: Pain Control (Acetaminophen, NSAIDs, Opioids if needed). Treat underlying cause. Wound care PRN.
DISPOSITION & FOLLOW-UP
  • Consider Admission: Systemic sx (fever, sepsis), Severe pain, Rapid progression, Extensive blistering/sloughing, Immunocompromised, Need IV Rx, Diagnostic uncertainty w/ concern, Social factors.
  • Discharge Instructions: Clear diagnosis (if possible), Treatment plan, Explicit Return Precautions (worsening rash, fever, new sx), Follow-up plan (PCP, Dermatology, Specific Clinic).
  • Consults: Dermatology, Infectious Disease, Rheumatology, Hematology, Surgery, Burn Unit as indicated by suspected diagnosis.
This flowchart is a clinical decision support tool intended for Emergency Department use by qualified healthcare professionals. It does not replace clinical judgment, comprehensive patient evaluation, or consideration of individual patient factors (allergies, comorbidities, age, weight, renal/hepatic function).
